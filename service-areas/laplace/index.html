What LaPlace does to a paint job

Four things that change the work here, and what each one means for the schedule and the price.

01

Water on two sides

The river to the south and the lake to the north. That is a lot of moisture in the air for a strip of land this narrow, and it is why start times here move more than they do inland.

02

Flood history is a paint problem too

Where a house has taken water, the substrate underneath is the question and the color on top is not. That gets checked before anything is quoted.

03

Neither the river nor the lake is salt

The Mississippi is fresh water and Lake Pontchartrain is brackish, which means the driver behind almost every failure we see here is humidity and mildew, not corrosion.

04

Storm season

June 1 to November 30, and this is a parish that takes it seriously.

Why the quote says two

A coat and a job are different things

Painting a house here is a different job from painting one in a dry climate. Humidity changes how long a coat stays open, and it decides how much film you need on a wall before the weather starts taking it back.

First coat

Bond and coverage

The first coat is doing a different job from the second. It bonds to what is underneath and it tells us where the substrate is still drinking, which is information you only get once paint is actually on the wall.

Second coat

Color and film build

The second coat is where the color goes true and the film reaches the thickness printed on the tin. One coat over a sound wall looks fine from the driveway and looks thin on the north side by spring.

My house took water in a storm. Can you just repaint it?

Not until we know the substrate is sound and genuinely dry underneath. Coating over material that still holds moisture traps that moisture in place, and the finish then fails from behind, not from the weather. We check first. And we tell you straight if it is not ready yet.

Why do start times move so much here?

LaPlace has the river on one side and the lake on the other. Overnight humidity runs high, and the surface has to clear the dew point margin before a coating goes on.

When is the best time of year to paint in LaPlace?

Autumn through spring gives the widest window by a distance. Summer is workable with early starts and a willingness to stop when the conditions turn on you mid-afternoon.

How often should I repaint?

Stucco and aluminum siding about every 5 to 6 years, painted brick 15 to 20.

Can you match the paint color already on the walls?

Yes. For most projects our paint specialists can match almost any color on your home from a chip or a wet sample. If an exact match is not possible we will talk you through the next best option.

How often should I paint?

It varies by material and exposure. Stucco every 5 to 6 years. Painted brick 15 to 20 years. Aluminum siding every 5 to 6 years. A wall that takes afternoon sun will always come due first.

Do I need to move furniture before the crew arrives?

No. Our team covers and protects furniture and floors. If a piece is delicate or awkward to move, it is worth taking it out of the room beforehand.

Is the second coat included in the price you quote?

Yes. Every estimate is written for two coats over prepared surface, and the number you get is the number for the finished job. If a surface genuinely only needs one, we say so and the quote comes down instead of staying where it was.
