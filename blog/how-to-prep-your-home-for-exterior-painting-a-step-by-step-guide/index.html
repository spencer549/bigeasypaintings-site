Key takeaways

  • Ideal exterior painting conditions run 45 to 90°F with 40% to 50% humidity.
  • A properly prepped exterior coat should hold up 5 to 7 years in this climate.
  • Lighter, heat-reflecting satin or semi-gloss finishes perform best against New Orleans UV.
  • Fall scheduling gives a new coat the best chance to cure fully before summer humidity returns.

Why Prep Work is Essential for Exterior Painting

Proper preparation is the foundation for a successful exterior painting project. It ensures that the paint adheres well, addresses existing issues, and provides a smooth and lasting finish.

Skipping or rushing through prep work can lead to peeling, cracking, and uneven paint application, resulting in the need for premature repainting.

That’s why preparation is essential to save cost in getting repaint and repairs in the future as well as have a more durable coating and protection needed for your home .

Step 1: Assess the Exterior

Begin by assessing the current condition of your home’s exterior. Look for any signs of damage, decay, or wear and tear. Take note of areas that may need special attention, such as peeling paint, cracks, or wood rot. A thorough assessment will guide your prep work and help you determine the necessary repairs.

Step 2: Clean the Surface

Cleanliness is essential for proper paint adhesion. Pressure wash or hand scrub the exterior surfaces to remove dirt, grime, mildew, and loose paint. Pay special attention to areas around windows, doors, and corners. Allow the surface to dry completely before moving on to the next step.

Step 3: Repair and Replace

Address any identified issues from your assessment. Fill in cracks and holes with a suitable filler, replace rotted or damaged wood, and fix any structural issues. Ensure that the surface is smooth and in good condition before proceeding to the next step.

Step 4: Protect Surfaces and Landscape

Before you start painting, take measures to protect surrounding surfaces and landscape features. Cover windows, doors, and any areas you don’t want to be painted with plastic or drop cloths. Use painter’s tape to protect edges and trim. Cover plants, shrubs, and other landscaping elements to prevent paint splatter.

Step 5: Prime the Surface

Applying a primer is essential, especially for surfaces with repairs or new surfaces. Primer creates a uniform base, improves paint adhesion, and enhances durability.Determine the right paint for your surface since metal, wood or masonry has different types of paint needed.

Step 6: Choose and Apply Paint

Choosing the right paint for your home requires a lot of consideration as the result will depend on the choice you made. The durability, appearance and protection of your home can greatly be affected so choose wisely and consider some important factors .

  • Climate and Weather Conditions : Choose a paint that is suitable for our Gulf Coast climate. Around New Orleans that means high humidity, intense summer heat, and heavy rain, so opt for a paint built to withstand those conditions. Consider paints with UV resistance to prevent fading and damage from the sun.
  • Surface Material : Different surface materials require different types of paint. Whether your home is made of wood, stucco, brick, or other materials, select a paint that is designed for optimal adhesion and protection on that specific surface.
  • Paint Finish : The finish you choose affects both aesthetics and maintenance. Flat or matte finishes hide imperfections but may be harder to clean. Satin and semi-gloss finishes are more durable and easier to clean, making them suitable for high-traffic areas.
  • Color and Aesthetics : Consider the architectural style of your home and the neighborhood when choosing a paint color. Some historical or neighborhood associations may have guidelines regarding exterior colors. Keep in mind that darker colors absorb more heat and may fade faster than lighter ones.
  • Quality of Paint : Invest in high-quality paint to ensure longevity and resistance to elements. Quality paints often contain better pigments, binders, and additives that contribute to better coverage, adhesion, and durability. Check for products with a good warranty, which can be an indicator of the manufacturer’s confidence in their product.
  • Primer : Depending on the condition of your exterior surfaces, using a primer may be necessary. Primer helps paint adhere better and can prevent issues like peeling and flaking. Certain surfaces, such as bare wood or new stucco, almost always require a primer.
  • Application Method : The method of application (brush, roller, or spray) can impact the finish and overall appearance of the paint. Sprayers may be more efficient for large, smooth surfaces, while brushes and rollers are better for detailed areas. Follow the manufacturer’s recommendations for the application method and tools.
  • Environmental Impact : Consider eco-friendly or low-VOC (volatile organic compounds) paint options, especially if you or your family members are sensitive to fumes or if you want to minimize your environmental impact.
  • Budget : While it’s essential to choose quality paint, also consider your budget. Higher-quality paints may have a higher upfront cost but can save money in the long run by requiring less frequent repainting.
  • Professional vs. DIY Application : Assess your own skill level and the scope of the project. Some homeowners prefer to hire professionals for large or complex exterior painting projects, while others choose to DIY. The decision may also depend on the type of surface and height of the building.

Step 7: Clean Up and Final Touches

Once the paint is dry, carefully remove any protective coverings and painter’s tape. Clean your painting tools promptly to ensure they are ready for future use. Inspect the painted surfaces for any touch-ups or additional coats that may be needed.

Step 8: Maintain Your Newly Painted Exterior

To extend the life of your exterior paint job, perform regular maintenance. Keep an eye out for signs of peeling or damage, and address any issues promptly. Clean the exterior periodically to remove dirt and prevent mildew growth.
